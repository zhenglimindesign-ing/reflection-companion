# Source-first diary and review composition

Use after resolving sources, recording detail and output options, before composing any completed diary/review. Work transiently in the current context; this workflow does not authorize saving a private plan, source copy or receipt. Use the selected personal destination only when saving is authorized. A body-only request hides the working map, not the audit.

## 1. Extract, group, then select

Read the in-scope original material and corrections. Make a compact working map: source ID/date, speaker, **exact supporting excerpt**, corrected facts, expressed meaning, useful historical AI contribution, and genuinely unknown outcomes. For period reviews, attach the last explicitly supported date to each factual status claim, not just to the thread; different claims in one thread may have different last-known dates. Keep these as separate fields rather than a draft narrative.

Group related exchanges into episodes or sustained questions before choosing prose. Several exercises, definitions or follow-ups may support one learning thread; they need not become separate diary items. A multi-topic message may still contain distinct worthwhile threads. Apply the [recording criteria](artifacts.md) to those threads, then choose details *within* each retained thread: keep what makes its experience, question, reasoning or correction understandable and worth revisiting. A language-learning thread can retain the frustration and a useful distinction without reproducing a glossary or every teaching example. The same selection applies to historical AI answers. `detailed` adds supported context within eligible threads; it does not turn them into transcripts. Do not force a topic count, word quota or common psychological explanation.

Account for routine lookups, repeated mechanics and acknowledgments as editorial omissions, without supplying a resolution status. A record used as evidence may still contain details reasonably left out; its ID being included proves neither that every subtopic survived nor that all of them should. Distinguish such selection from a missing meaningful thread or unread source.

For supported dated Markdown notes, obtain the index with `scripts/source_notes.py --source <authorized note> --ids <in-scope IDs>`. Extract excerpts from that index before writing. Otherwise use actual messages and their metadata directly; do not create a local source export merely to use this helper.

Corrections change the factual map before prose is written. Nearby statements do not supply a causal relationship. No time of day, companion, motive, acceptance or result in the excerpt means no such detail in factual narration. A missing outcome is unknown, not a failed or continuing task. When compressing an unknown, retain the source's knowledge/verification verb: “not checked” cannot become “does not exist,” and “not received a reply” cannot become “they have nothing to say.” Likewise, “not intending to make a plan” does not prove “never made a plan.” Keep the status clause close to the original wording; knowledge, intention, action and outcome are different claims. In a period comparison, keep each supported state at its actual observed date. A later record without a new statement about it does not prove that the earlier state continued, stopped or was never reframed. Retain the user's own feeling/thinking verb: considering a question does not establish worry, and a pleasant moment does not establish relief.

## 2. Compose within each narrator's evidence

Organize the selected threads into readable thematic paragraphs. Preserve an agreed archive layout; its two voices can each use the same brief topic labels when helpful. Keep related experience, meaning and uncertainty together in a paragraph, rather than start a new block for every sentence or tool evidence unit. Let each paragraph carry an experience or line of thought, rather than repeat “I asked / the AI answered” for each exchange. For each factual sentence, retain its supporting excerpt in the working map. Start each user paragraph from the selected original sentences; edit their order, remove repetition and connect supported clauses. Prefer retaining the source wording over adding a smoother but ungrounded scene. Paraphrase the supported meaning; do not write an attractive scene first and find loose source associations afterward. A heading can connect topics for navigation without giving them a shared psychological cause.

- **User-attributed claims:** in a diary narrative or a review's facts/shifts, build claims about what the user felt, understood, chose or did from their own supported excerpts. This applies to “I,” “you” and third person alike. An explanation appearing only in a historical AI answer stays in the AI passage; a nearby user acknowledgment or acceptance of another distinction does not establish adoption of every clause. Expand detailed writing with existing user detail and reasoning, or with separately attributed useful AI answers. Sparse material may produce an equally short entry. Keep an unresolved question unresolved.
- **Historical AI:** label the condensed passage as an earlier AI answer/correction/suggestion, and address the user as “you.” A clearly labeled historical-AI section can establish attribution once; repeated “the earlier AI answered” is not needed in every paragraph. Select its useful distinction, reason, correction or limit, rather than replaying the user's scene or listing all answers and withdrawals. An earlier AI idea remains an idea unless the user adopted it. A choice about one item does not extend to an adjacent AI proposal. Keep the object of a decision or commitment in the user's source wording; “both” or “the above items” can silently widen it.
- **New AI observation:** include only when enabled, separately labeled and tentative. Do not put a new explanation in the user's “I” even with “maybe.” Do not relabel an earlier answer as a new observation, or a new interpretation as an earlier answer.

Example of the boundary (not a template to copy): a user says “It was raining when I left; I waited by the door.” “I waited by the door because it was raining” adds a causal claim; “The rain helped me enjoy a quiet pause” adds a feeling and interpretation. Keep the observed rain and waiting without either addition. Richer prose is useful only when it preserves the available meaning.

## 3. Audit the actual draft, then repair

Compare **every factual clause**, including modifiers and implied relationships, with its own narrator's excerpt, rather than accept an evidence association for the paragraph as a whole. First separate user claims from historical AI contributions: if a phrase about the user's understanding or choice occurs only in an AI explanation, remove it from the user/facts passage or keep it as an attributed AI contribution. For a continuing or changed state, compare the clause's date with its own last supported date; do not inherit a nearby clause's newer date. Then ask for each remaining clause: does the source state this exact meaning; have I added when, why, with whom, how it felt, adoption, success or resolution? Compare the factual verbs and modifiers as well as the theme. Seeing an inscription on a bookshelf does not establish entering a library or opening a book. A plausible way the scene happened is still an additional event. If the edited verb/action is not supported, restore the source verb instead of treating it as stylistic filler. Evidence for two events does not support a relationship between them. Delete unsupported additions, or move a useful tentative interpretation to an enabled AI component. Do not patch it merely by adding “perhaps.”

For labeled dated Markdown sources, use the read-only `source_notes.py --source <file> --ids <IDs> --preview-draft` with JSON on stdin. Submit the actual candidate with its layout; the response includes the evidence comparison and mechanically checked `body` together. Read and semantically/editorially check the complete returned body before delivery. A correction discovered in this review belongs in the input, followed by a new preview and comparison; editing only the final reply leaves that correction unchecked. Deliver the latest reviewed `body` unchanged. A minimal payload is:

```json
{
  "sentences": [
    {"text": "A final factual sentence", "voice": "user", "evidence": [
      {"id": "source ID", "text": "exact labeled source excerpt"}
    ]}
  ],
  "omitted": ["source IDs omitted by editorial selection"],
  "language": "en",
  "source_appendix": "links",
  "title": "Plain title without a Markdown prefix",
  "sections": [{"heading": "Plain label", "voice": "user", "indices": [0]}]
}
```

Pass the JSON with a quoted heredoc or a Python subprocess's `input=json.dumps(payload)`; a Python code heredoc already consumes stdin and cannot also supply the helper's JSON input. Alternatively import this same read-only module: `read_notes(source_path, ids)` returns a **list of note dictionaries** and `preview_draft(notes, payload)` returns an **audit dictionary with `body`**. Its `comparison`, `source_scope` and candidate are returned for your final semantic/editorial review, not an automated verdict. These functions perform the same checks as the CLI and write no files. An invocation/input error requires repair, not skipping a supported check. Do not silently export private material to make invocation easier.

Use `voice: user`, `historical_ai` or `new_ai`; excerpts include their original speaker label (`用户：`, `用户更正：`, `历史 AI 答复：` / `建议：` / `更正：`, or corresponding `User:` / `Historical AI:` labels). A record with several speakers must use the excerpt for the actual owner. Supply the **actual draft's factual sentences**, not a shortened stand-in. Clearly attributed exact quotations may use the existing `--verify` quote check separately; do not rewrite them as AI paraphrases. The preview returns the candidate alongside its verbatim evidence, rejects out-of-scope/missing excerpts and mismatched labeled speakers, flags an unquoted user “I” in a historical-AI summary, cues a few potentially added user affect words with a conservative literal check (skipped for script-changing paraphrases), flags possible loss of a knowledge/intention qualifier in a negative paraphrase, and cues completion wording absent from the user excerpts. A reported action alone does not establish completion of its wider task; restore its action verb when completion is unsupported. This last check is conservative: compare the actual clause; an explicitly sourced absence remains valid. Prefer restoring the source wording when compression has changed its meaning. Read that comparison and repair the prose; `ok` validates these mechanical checks only, not semantic entailment or completeness. Recheck changed sentences. **A tool pass on an earlier draft does not cover rewritten final prose.** Other source formats, unsupported speaker labels/languages or an unavailable tool require the same direct comparison, not a claimed tool pass.

Check selected dates and original quotes with `--verify` too when applicable. Then do a separate editorial read of the full artifact: can the reader recover the worthwhile experiences and thinking without reconstructing the chat sequence? Compare it with the working map for missing meaningful threads/corrections, duplicated user stories in the AI part, catalogues of examples, repeated conclusions and disabled components. Repair these in the actual draft and repeat the factual comparison. A faithful transcript-like or audit-like artifact still fails this editorial check. Do not output the working map or tool receipt as the diary.

The preview uses the existing renderer and its safeguards. Each `text` is a paragraph; it can contain several source-backed sentences with evidence for every factual clause. Keep related material together rather than making each evidence unit a paragraph. `title` and `heading` are plain single-line labels without `#`; the renderer supplies Markdown syntax. After checking the whole returned body and its comparison, use `body` unchanged. With `source_appendix: agreed_originals`, continue using the separate agreed-originals appendix/export workflow; this small preview handles links/off only.

**Compatible two-step interface:** existing callers may use `--audit-draft` with only `sentences`, `omitted`, `language`, `source_appendix`, then review the comparison and call `--render-draft` on the same helper: add `title` (plain single-line label without `#`, empty when the user wants no title), `sections` and `audit_sha256` (the `draft_sha256` returned by the successful audit) to the checked payload. Each section contains `heading` (plain label without `#`, empty for free prose), `voice` and `indices` (zero-based indices into `sentences`). The renderer supplies heading syntax and makes each `text` a paragraph. A `text` may contain several source-backed sentences in one thematic paragraph; include evidence for every factual clause in it. Do not equate one evidence unit with one paragraph or create a new sentence after checking. All sentences must appear exactly once and match the section's narrator. Read the returned comparison as a semantic pass before rendering: check the subject, action, object and status in each actual clause, especially a user choice beside an AI suggestion. The renderer requires the prior source-and-draft digest, reruns the mechanical checks and returns the final Markdown, including the enabled bounded source note. **Use this returned body unchanged as the final artifact; do not switch pronouns or recompose it during delivery.** If prose needs improvement, edit its source-backed payload, run the audit again, review the new comparison and render again. The digest binds the source and text between these steps; it is not a semantic approval or an authorization token. With `agreed_originals`, use the draft audit and the existing agreed-originals appendix/export workflow; this small renderer does not assemble an originals archive. For other formats, make the equivalent final-text comparison after the last edit; a checked stand-in is insufficient.

## 4. Keep provenance factual and secondary

With `source_appendix` enabled, use the audit's `processing_note` for supported labeled Markdown input. It states the supplied date range, limited scope and editorial selection; it never asserts omitted queries were answered or completed. The full ID inventory remains in `source_scope` and the evidence comparison, not the visible footer. Keep the note unchanged except for a verified source link or a necessary coverage gap. For other sources, use the same bounded pattern: “Material: [actually read source range and limits]. Editing: selected by topic; omitted [category] as an editorial choice.” Do not explain a category by guessing outcomes, significance to the user's whole life or absence from unavailable chats.

Historical AI attribution belongs next to its passage even when the source appendix is off. An enabled footer is part of a completed artifact, including an output-only request. With `source_appendix: off`, omit it entirely. A consequential retrieval gap belongs early; a selection note does not replace gap disclosure. Routine provenance is a short paragraph or a link to the authorized source/progress area. Source-ID lists, counts, tool steps, versions, receipts and checkpoints belong in that separate audit area only when authorized; body-only output does not authorize creating it. Keep the narrative the main reading experience. A save/readback claim still needs real verification, whether or not its receipt is shown.
