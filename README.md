# Reflection Companion

[简体中文](README.zh-CN.md) | **English**

**Not to help AI understand you better — to help you understand yourself better.**

Reflection Companion is an AI companion for reflection. It turns experiences, conversations, and traces of thinking you already have into material you can revisit: to notice change, surface patterns in your thinking that you may not have noticed yourself, examine your judgments, explore another perspective, and keep something useful when you choose to.

Use it for a diary, a weekly review, or a period or project retrospective. It can also compare material over time to surface possible recurring patterns or blind spots, help you reconsider a question, or introduce a new way to explore yourself.

**Public Alpha · v0.5.2**

Available for Codex, Claude Code, and Claude web.

---

## 1. Why Reflection Companion exists

“Know thyself” is an old instruction.

What is new is how much material many of us now leave behind while thinking with AI.

We talk with AI about work, relationships, interests, choices, uncertainty, and plans. Over time, those conversations contain more than answers. They also preserve traces of:

- how we frame a problem;
- which criteria we repeatedly use to decide;
- where we tend to get stuck;
- which explanations we reach for;
- what kind of evidence changes our mind;
- and whether something that once mattered still does.

Some patterns become visible only when experiences from different moments are placed next to each other. You may never explicitly ask, “Do I respond to uncertainty in the same way every time?”

**Often, the first step toward change is not finding an immediate solution, but noticing what is actually happening.**

Most of this material becomes dormant chat history after the immediate problem is solved.

Reflection Companion helps bring it back into use.

The aim is not to generate a fixed personality profile or let AI decide what your life means. It is to help you look more clearly at your own experience: **what happened, what is changing, which ways of thinking may be recurring, what your judgments rest on, and what is still worth thinking about.**

Your conversations are not the whole of you. But they can be useful material for understanding yourself.

## 2. What it can help you do

### 2.1 Revisit experience and change

Connect thinking from different moments instead of merely summarizing recent activity.

You may notice that:

- something that once felt urgent no longer does;
- you now use different criteria for the same decision;
- some parts changed while others remained surprisingly stable;
- or a question that used to be unresolved has quietly reached an answer.

Diaries, weekly reviews, monthly reflections, and project retrospectives are all versions of this job.

### 2.2 Notice patterns you may not have seen yourself

From inside a situation, we usually see only the current moment.

But similar concerns, decision habits, reactions, or questions may be scattered across conversations that happened weeks or months apart.

When there is enough evidence, Reflection Companion can connect those fragments and propose a **pattern worth checking**:

- When things are uncertain, do I tend to keep preparing rather than begin?
- Do I often assume I have fallen short before looking for evidence?
- Across several different choices, am I protecting the same underlying thing?
- Do apparently unrelated problems rely on the same decision criterion?

These are not diagnoses or conclusions about who you are.

One repetition does not establish a pattern, and a lack of counterexamples does not prove one.

The Companion should distinguish evidence from interpretation and your own confirmation. You can reject an observation, qualify it, or revise it as new experience appears.

### 2.3 See your own thinking more clearly

Sometimes another piece of advice is not what you need.

It may be more useful to examine the explanation you are already using.

Reflection Companion can help ask:

- Am I treating an assumption as a fact?
- Is there another explanation that fits the evidence?
- When I say “I always do this,” is that actually supported?
- Which condition am I leaving out?
- Am I still solving the same problem I started with?

The goal is not to disagree with you.

Your original judgment may turn out to be sound. The point is to make the judgment itself clearer.

### 2.4 Encounter something outside your current frame

Sometimes the useful move is not deeper analysis, but a different direction.

Reflection Companion can introduce a relevant concept, perspective, question, experience, or exploration method — and explain why it may connect to what you are thinking about.

This is not random inspiration. The aim is to open a part of the landscape you were not already looking at.

### 2.5 Let useful learning continue

Some things deserve to survive beyond one conversation:

a decision that genuinely changed, an earlier interpretation that was corrected, a question that remains open, or a recurring pattern that may be worth checking again.

When you explicitly choose to keep something, Reflection Companion can preserve it for later relevant conversations.

New experience can also overturn an old interpretation.

**What is kept is not “the truth about you.” It is material that can remain inspectable, revisable, and correctable.**

## 3. Start from what is happening now

There are no commands to memorize and no profile to complete before you begin.

Describe what you want in ordinary language.

| If you want to… | You might say |
| --- | --- |
| Reflect on a day | “Help me look back on what was worth remembering today — what mattered, and whether anything actually changed. Ask me about gaps if needed.” |
| Review a week | “Review this week without just listing what I did. What judgments changed, what reached an outcome, and what is still worth thinking about?” |
| Revisit a period or project | “Look back on this project from the beginning. How was I thinking at first, which judgments changed later, and which criteria stayed consistent?” |
| Look for recurring patterns | “Looking across the recent discussions you can actually access, is there any way of thinking that keeps showing up but I may not have noticed? Only use supported evidence and treat it as something to test.” |
| Examine a judgment | “I keep telling myself that I am avoiding this. Do not simply agree with that interpretation — help me look at the evidence and other plausible explanations.” |
| Explore yourself differently | “What are a few different self-exploration approaches I could try? Give me distinct options, and start one after I choose.” |
| Keep one small moment | “I want to remember this small thing from today. I do not need much analysis.” |

The same Companion can move naturally between these situations.

### 3.1 Reflection can also happen proactively

If your host supports scheduled tasks, you do not have to remember to initiate every review yourself.

You can choose to set up things such as:

- an evening invitation to reflect briefly on the day;
- a recurring diary draft based only on the agreed accessible material;
- a weekly review of changes and unresolved questions;
- a regular suggestion for a new self-exploration exercise.

Scheduling is opt-in. You choose the scope, timing, and saving behavior.

Installing Reflection Companion **does not create schedules automatically**. Support for scheduled execution, source access, and unattended delivery varies by host; see the platform documentation for the verified scope.

## 4. Different time scales, different kinds of reflection

**A day, week, month, quarter and year should not be the same summary stretched across different dates.** As the time span changes, so does what a useful reflection needs to notice and connect: a diary stays close to lived experience; longer reviews compare evidence over time, revisit judgments, and look for patterns together with their limits.

| Period | What the review looks for |
| --- | --- |
| Day | Concrete scenes, your expressed feelings and moments worth keeping. |
| Week | What happened, which judgments changed and which observations need checking. |
| Month | Threads with stronger evidence, where attention went and explanations to revise. |
| Quarter | Direction across months, possible patterns and the conditions that limit them. |
| Year | Chapters, choices, ordinary life and wishes without a compulsory growth story. |

The following excerpts are authored from one fictional source set, not a real person’s diary or host-test results.

**A diary can keep a small evening:**

> The rain stopped on my way home. I walked an extra stop along the river. This evening is worth recording: “I didn't produce anything tonight, but I was living.”

**A week can connect evidence and correct a judgment:**

> With three signups, you considered cancelling the book group. All three later confirmed, and Xiaohe offered to bring a friend. That separates the size of a gathering from people's willingness to attend. It does not prove that attendance no longer matters to you; it adds evidence beside the earlier judgment.

[Read all five completed examples and an analysis-off variant](plugins/reflection-companion/skills/reflection-companion/assets/examples/reviews.en.md).

These structures are adjustable starting points, not fixed forms. You can keep more of your original wording, ask for a more natural diary or review, or reduce analysis; AI observations and questions can also be turned off. See the [user guide](docs/USER_GUIDE.md) for detailed output choices and saving rules.

### 5.1 Model and reasoning starting point

Use your host's current general-purpose model at its default reasoning effort. In Codex, **GPT-6.1 Sol** is a starting option when available in your model menu. For Claude, use an available general-purpose model; the Skill does not require a particular vendor or model.

A short diary from a few notes is a useful first trial. For a review spanning many sources or months, try **high** effort if the model drops earlier evidence, misses corrections or confuses statements with interpretation after the source coverage is checked. This is a practical starting guideline, not a benchmark ranking or a requirement to use the highest setting.

Higher effort takes longer and uses more tokens; it cannot recover history the host cannot access. First check sources and the written result, then adjust effort. The assistant should not change your model or settings merely because this README recommends a starting point. See [OpenAI's model and reasoning guidance](https://developers.openai.com/codex/models).

## 6. Sources, saving, and boundaries

Reflection Companion can only use material the current platform can **actually access**, plus material you choose to provide or authorize.

Installing the Skill does **not**:

- grant access to all of your conversations across platforms;
- enable personal saving automatically;
- create recurring schedules automatically;
- or add your private answers to the public exploration catalog.

History, file access, and continuity differ across hosts. When a reflection depends on previous material, the Companion should say what it actually covered rather than pretending it read a complete archive.

For a request to backfill a whole period, it should check discovery and source-text coverage, attempt supported retrieval repairs, and report any remaining gap before calling the archive complete. A recent list or an exhausted cursor alone does not prove full history. Existing records are baselines; new outputs should have their own verified links. If you ask for comparison copies, originals remain unchanged.

Personal diaries, quotations and source indexes belong in your chosen private destination. A product repository's output or scratch folder is not a default personal archive, even when the repository is private. The Companion should finish the authorized writing, saving and readback work, then tell you what remains or that no further action is needed.

Its interpretations are not final either.

You can always say:

- “That does not sound like me.”
- “You are missing an important condition.”
- “I do not think this is a stable pattern.”
- “Do not save this.”
- “Stop.”

If local continuity is enabled, saved material should retain provenance, status, and corrections, and remain inspectable, editable, and deletable.

Reflection Companion is not a diagnostic or therapy tool, and it should not turn sparse evidence into fixed personality labels.

**Meaning remains yours.**

## 7. Does self-exploration content change?

Yes — but not through an automated scraper collecting prompts from the web.

Reflection Companion includes a bundled set of exploration methods, can adapt an exercise to the situation already under discussion, and can read a curated public exploration catalog when you explicitly want something new.

The public catalog can be updated independently of a Skill release.

Those updates are still editorial: candidates are found, sources checked, mechanisms compared against existing material, rewritten, and tested before publication. There is no automatic collection or publishing service.

Your private answers never become part of that public catalog.

Browse the current bundled library:

[Self-exploration library](https://github.com/zhenglimindesign-ing/reflection-companion/blob/main/docs/PROMPTS.md)
