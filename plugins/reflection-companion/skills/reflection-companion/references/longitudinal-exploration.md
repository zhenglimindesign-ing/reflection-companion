# Open-ended exploration from personal history

Use for broad questions about recurring patterns, blind spots, happiness, self-understanding or present priorities. Distinguish discovery from testing an already supplied hypothesis. In discovery, choose sources before settling on an interpretation; derive candidate patterns after broad organization of the available material. In hypothesis testing, the supplied proposition may guide relevant retrieval, including counterevidence.

## Establish what the sources can cover

Reuse verified source inventories and unchanged retrieved material. Locate the user's original conversations or notes, any existing diaries, and summaries that may help navigate them. Record each source's origin, access surface, date range, text/boundary status and material gaps. An AI summary can guide retrieval or be compared with original text; it is not independent confirmation of its own interpretation. User-confirmed material has authority only at the scope actually confirmed. Do not claim access to a host's private memory synthesis merely because a prior answer sounds comprehensive.

Build a time/topic coverage map from dated messages. Keep discovered sources, fetched conversations, returned messages and boundary-verified transcripts separate. Counts are retrieval facts, not measures of representativeness; a coverage percentage needs a verified denominator. Metadata may identify likely topics, but personal claims require inspecting actual content. Mark sparse periods and domains as sparse rather than absent from the user's life.

For browser transcripts, bind each extracted body to its own user/message container and displayed date before using it. Accessibility text may omit long bodies: never scan past the next role heading to fill a missing message. A page node index is not an original message ID. Preserve ambiguous receipts as pending, exclude their inferred dates and roles from analysis, and verify them against the message container or another original source. Do not manufacture IDs or timestamps to satisfy a source check.

Choose time bounds for the question and explain their basis. Recent circumstances help assess present priorities; longer patterns need earlier baselines and evidence of continuity or change. Source availability may constrain the window, but does not turn that window into the user's relevant life history. When the user specifies no period, first inspect accessible history and its distribution; do not silently apply a recent-month default. If an earlier archive or outcome could materially change the interpretation, repair that gap through supported retrieval under [source readiness](source-readiness.md).

Do not set a hidden chat-count cap, choose only emotionally vivid conversations, or search only vocabulary that supports the first impression. Consider the scoped material across time and ordinary as well as difficult situations. Broad reading can proceed in batches. Full account reading is not mandatory for every reflection: select proportionately to the breadth of the claim, explain consequential exclusions, and use coverage/gaps to decide where further reading can change the answer. Exhausted cursors and repeated AI summaries do not establish completeness or corroboration.

## Continue when sources are incomplete

Define an earlier baseline by the comparison it enables, not a fixed month or cutoff: it may be a prior stable period, a different life stage, or earlier messages in an already discovered conversation. Use known originals, supported dated search and browser reads where available. An account export is an optional retrieval route; it is a prerequisite only when the requested account-wide coverage cannot be established through other authorized surfaces. Do not imply that old messages can only be read from an export.

Separate each requested judgment from its source dependency. Explain which missing period, context or outcome could materially change that judgment. Current recommendations and provisional observations can use sufficient recent evidence with their actual dates and limits; lifelong patterns require evidence across relevant stages. Recommendations about the quickest or largest benefit remain proposals to test, even after more history is obtained. A source-check `blocked` result limits a completeness claim for its declared corpus; it does not block the source audit, an explicitly provisional answer or independent Skill implementation and delivery.

Repair specific gaps that can change the answer, reuse verified text and stop equivalent failed reads. Deliver supported parts without repeatedly asking the user to approve ordinary work or treating silence as acceptance of a narrower final scope. Keep the original unmet request visible, name only the dependent judgments as pending, and state the next supported retrieval or minimum user input. If the needed evidence cannot be obtained, an unresolved or unanswerable judgment is a valid outcome; do not manufacture a discovery, insist on a full export for every reflection, or leave all independent deliverables waiting. Source/mechanism audit can be delivered now and updated with later evidence. Keep implementation, installation and host reload checks separate from personal-history repair.

## Organize before judging

First organize events, expressed needs, actions, corrections and reported outcomes by period and topic, without requiring them to fit one theme. Keep direct user statements separate from pasted advice, reported opinions, automated output and the assistant's interpretation. A source can matter without being emotionally intense or frequently mentioned. Repetition within one episode is not evidence that a pattern persists across years or contexts.

Then derive candidate patterns and compare supporting examples, counterexamples, later corrections and alternative explanations. When combining evidence across domains, check that the same claimed process is present; similar emotional words do not establish a shared mechanism. A question about competence, safety, respect or resources is not automatically a statement about global self-worth. Preserve the user's clarified distinction rather than replacing it with a preferred interpretation. Active research or quality control is not automatically avoidance, anxiety or inability to act. Inspect the interaction as well as the user: repeated clarification may be caused by inaccurate, inconsistent or unnecessarily restrictive assistant advice. A painful transition does not establish a stable trait. A missing outcome does not prove that an issue remains unresolved. Retire an attractive explanation when the evidence does not sustain it.

Check whether the user already articulated a proposed blind spot in earlier material. Do not present prior self-awareness as a newly discovered hidden pattern. Distinguish knowing a pattern, difficulty acting on it and lacking practical support; further questions alone do not establish which is happening. Likewise, interests or reported activities cannot disprove the user's stated difficulty feeling happy.

Scale the answer to what was observed. "Quickest" and "largest" happiness changes are recommendations to test unless actual comparative outcomes support ranking them; reading more chats alone cannot establish causality. Offer a small relevant experiment where useful. For lifelong blind spots or inaccurate self-understanding, distinguish an observed discrepancy from a speculative motive. Current priorities should consider the latest confirmed constraints and actions, rather than simply the most frequent topic.

## Make the reasoning auditable

For consequential judgments, provide the relevant dated original sources, the inference, material counterevidence or alternatives, and what remains unknown. Keep the body readable; put a larger source/time map and exclusions in an appendix when requested. Explain why these data sources, dates and additional reads were selected. Account for earlier and later evidence and avoid counting derived diaries as new independent observations.

If broad requested history remains unavailable, report the source audit and supported provisional observations with an explicit boundary. Do not silently narrow a lifelong question to a recent profile or call the broader task complete. Finish independent authorized work, name the specific remaining source/access gap and explain how it could change the judgment. Do not make the user restate material already retrievable or approve ordinary retrieval repeatedly.
