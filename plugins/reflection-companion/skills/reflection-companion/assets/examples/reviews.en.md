> “I didn't produce anything tonight, but I was living.”

I had planned a book-review video for the weekend. I have not decided yet, so I will not promise it.

## 2. Weekly review

**Fictional weekly review · 2025-09-22–28 · A small group, real interest**

### 2.1 Facts · What moved in the world

With three signups, you considered cancelling the book group. All three later confirmed, and Xiaohe offered to bring a friend. The gathering went ahead. You found you could listen rather than keep the conversation going. The stopped rain and extra walk along the river also belong in this week's record.

At work, handing over a demo with noncore pages removed brought a concrete response: a colleague understood the main workflow's entry point differently. The animation you had kept changing did not address that issue. The notes identify a problem; they do not report that it was fixed.

### 2.2 Shifts · A judgment revised

On September 22, a small turnout felt like failure. On September 24, you corrected the facts:

> “My worry is that the numbers look unimpressive, not that nobody is interested.”

That separates the size of a gathering from people's willingness to attend. The next evening added an experience worth remembering. It does not prove that attendance no longer matters to you; it adds evidence beside the earlier judgment.

### 2.3 Signals · An observation to check

Before the event you counted people; afterwards you thought of publishing a recap so it would count as an outcome. Taken together, these notes suggest a limited possibility: for this book group, a visible result may help you judge whether it was worth arranging.

That observation needs a boundary. On September 20 you rejected the broader AI claim that you always prove your worth through outcomes, distinguishing work delivery from hobbies. This review should not revive that label or extend a week's experience to all of life.

### 2.4 Open questions · What remains unknown

There is no follow-up showing whether the entry-point problem was fixed. You have not yet said whether you want to keep hosting a small book group.

The video is in a different state: you chose not to give it a release date next week. It can stay open without being tracked as procrastination or a missing deliverable.

### 2.5 Experiment · An optional small trial

If you want, after the next gathering you could record just one moment you want to keep. That would test whether the experience itself supplies another way to evaluate the event. It needs no published recap or additional report.

What made this evening feel worth remembering?

## 3. Monthly review

**Fictional monthly review · September 2025 · Different standards for delivery and living**

### 3.1 Two threads this month

At work, you revisited “validated” on September 10: two colleagues liked the presentation, but neither used it. On September 23, a stripped-down demo exposed a different understanding of the workflow entry point. Approval, identifying a problem and solving it are different results, and this record should preserve that distinction.

Outside work, you first wondered whether photography class could produce a portfolio. Later you liked an evening photographing the sky without selecting or publishing anything. The small book group and walk after the rain added other moments worth keeping.

### 3.2 What has firmer evidence

The workflow problem is specific, and you explicitly enjoyed several hobby experiences. They support different judgments: work needs further checking of the flow; an evening can matter to you without producing something for display.

There is no record of routine demo use or of the entry-point problem being fixed. Nor do these notes establish a lasting hobby habit. Positive feedback and good experiences retain their actual weight.

### 3.3 Attention and room for living

Attendance and the wish to show an outcome still drew your attention near month-end. Photographing the sky, listening to Xiaohe and walking an extra stop offered other experiences: sometimes you wanted an evening to be lived before giving it a purpose.

These moments appear in the notes. Without time-allocation evidence, they do not establish work-life balance.

### 3.4 An earlier explanation to retire

On September 20 you rejected the general claim about proving worth through results:

> “I want to tell apart the times that need delivery and the times that don't need to be measured by results.”

Needing a complete plan under a tight deadline and feeling unsuccessful about a small book group should not collapse into a personality judgment.

### 3.5 What to keep

Keep the correction to “validated,” the enjoyable evening sky, and the book group's aftermath:

> “I didn't produce anything tonight, but I was living.”

The video remains an interest, with no release date chosen. This review does not add a task for next month.

## 4. Quarterly review

**Fictional quarterly review · Q3 2025 · Different things need different standards**

### 4.1 The course of this period

In July, weekly planning helped work delivery and you carried signup targets into the book group. A friend came to read together, but you did not quite believe three people could make a good gathering.

In August, the outing was your favorite part of photography class. September brought evening skies, listening to others and the walk after rain. By quarter-end, you had a small gathering you wanted to remember. Attendance had not stopped mattering; there was more material with which to judge an event.

### 4.2 Which judgments hold, and where

Across these months, the hobby notes suggest that you sometimes first look for a portfolio, scale or a visible outcome to confirm an activity's value. Later experiences also let the activity itself enter the judgment.

The work notes limit that observation. July planning helped delivery; September's tight deadline called for a fuller plan; and you explicitly rejected treating all these needs as perfectionism. A more useful reading is that you are distinguishing standards by situation. “You finally stopped pursuing results” would misrepresent the evidence.

### 4.3 Investment and present needs

At work, the standard became more specific. By August's end you wanted to test the actual workflow; September separated presentation approval from a main-flow problem. Further records are needed to show whether changes worked.

In hobbies, some plans can remain without a delivery date. You still want the video but chose no release date next week. That is an arrangement choice, not evidence of abandoning creative work.

### 4.4 Things not to lose

The quarter holds more than methods: a friend wanting to read together, the photography outing, skies after fixing the camera, and being able to listen to Xiaohe. These can sit beside work judgments without a single results metric ranking everything.

> “I want to tell apart the times that need delivery and the times that don't need to be measured by results.”

### 4.5 An open direction

Work still needs a follow-up on the workflow problem. The book group and video can be reconsidered as new experiences arrive. The notes support no new quarterly goal system.

Which experience that you already enjoyed would you most like to keep in the next period?

## 5. Yearly review

**Fictional yearly review · 2025 · Beyond what can be shown**

This review sees portions of life that reached the page: early year, spring and summer, Q3, and December. It cannot reconstruct every month or judge the whole year's mood, relationships or work performance.

### 5.1 The year's first delivery list

In January you wanted something showable every month, with work and hobbies on the same list. By March, missed plans made a week feel wasted. A lovely hike with friends did not count among what you had gained.

Those notes show an earlier way of evaluating a week. They also preserve a fact easy to omit: the enjoyable weekend happened.

### 5.2 Spring and summer choices

In May, tired evenings led you to protect seeing friends and cooking. Fewer tasks still brought guilt, while you reported better sleep. In June, you removed half the hobby goals but kept the work goals:

> “I don't want to turn everything into a sprint.”

That change has boundaries. You adjusted hobbies and evenings without declaring work delivery unimportant.

### 5.3 Q3's different experiences

Work evidence grew more specific: presentation approval, feedback on real use and a problem being solved need distinct descriptions. Outside work were the photography outing, evening sky and three-person book group.

September's correction belongs here too. Work delivery and hobby activities should not be swept into “proving worth through outcomes.” An annual story must not compress those different experiences back into one label.

### 5.4 A tension still present at year-end

December records no complete transformation. You still ask what can be shown, while seeing friends, reading together and evenings you liked also feel worth keeping. Your view of work delivery remains.

One supported change is making room for different standards for different things. It is a reading of these notes, closer to your later distinction than January's shared delivery list; it is not a conclusion about your entire life. There is also too little material to recount all gains or losses. Missing parts stay blank.

### 5.5 What to carry forward

You chose neither an annual keyword nor new goals. The final note already states a wish:

> “I want to keep the book group and take the camera out sometimes. I'll decide the rest later.”

It can stay a wish rather than become a plan. Earlier expectations, later choices and remaining guilt can coexist in the year's record.

If you kept one concrete moment for your future self, which would it be?
